Slide 3 of 18 · Open in PDF
Easy explanation

Agile teams work in repeated cycles and collaborate to respond to new information. Self-organization and varied skills help them deliver useful work incrementally.

Example: A team can release a small improvement, gather feedback, and adapt the next cycle.

Key terms to remember
  • Iterative development: Delivering and refining work in repeated cycles so teams can adapt quickly to feedback and change.
Slide 4 of 18 · Open in PDF
Easy explanation

The Product Owner guides product value and backlog priorities; the Scrum Master supports the process and removes obstacles; the Development Team builds product increments.

Example: For a new feature, the Owner sets priority, the Scrum Master helps clear a blocker, and the team implements it.

Slide 5 of 18 · Open in PDF
Easy explanation

The Product Owner is accountable for maximizing the value delivered. Clear goals and a well-managed backlog help guide that work.

Example: They may prioritize a frequently requested improvement ahead of a minor enhancement.

Key terms to remember
  • Product Owner accountability: Singular accountability for maximizing delivered product value through effective backlog management and clear goals.
Slide 6 of 18 · Open in PDF
Easy explanation

The Product Owner creates and refines Product Backlog Items, orders them by value, and keeps the backlog clear and visible to stakeholders.

Example: A PBI might describe the ability to reset a forgotten password.

Key terms to remember
  • Product Backlog Items / PBIs: Clearly articulated work items that the Product Owner refines, prioritizes, and makes transparent.
Slide 7 of 18 · Open in PDF
Easy explanation

The Product Owner connects stakeholder needs to product goals so the Development Team understands what outcome it should build toward.

Example: Customer requests can be translated into a clear goal for the next product change.

Key terms to remember
  • Stakeholder engagement: The Product Owner linking customer and business needs to clear product goals for the Development Team.
Slide 8 of 18 · Open in PDF
Easy explanation

Backlog refinement is ongoing: the Product Owner incorporates feedback and changing needs so upcoming work stays relevant.

Example: New user feedback may cause a backlog item to be clarified or reprioritized.

Key terms to remember
  • Iterative backlog refinement: Continuously adjusting the backlog using feedback and changing market or stakeholder needs.
Slide 9 of 18 · Open in PDF
Easy explanation

The Scrum Master serves the team by facilitating Scrum, coaching Agile practices, removing impediments, and limiting distractions.

Example: If another group’s approval is blocking work, the Scrum Master helps resolve the dependency.

Key terms to remember
  • Scrum Master servant-leadership: Enabling the team through facilitation, coaching, impediment removal, and protection from distractions.
Slide 10 of 18 · Open in PDF
Easy explanation

The Scrum Master helps Scrum events happen on schedule and keeps discussions focused, timeboxed, and useful.

Example: During a review, the facilitator makes room for each participant and keeps the discussion on the topic.

Key terms to remember
  • Scrum event facilitation: Helping scheduled Scrum events remain productive, positive, timeboxed, and transparent.
Slide 11 of 18 · Open in PDF
Easy explanation

An impediment is something that slows or blocks progress. The Scrum Master helps the team address technical or organizational roadblocks.

Example: A missing test environment could be raised and coordinated as a team impediment.

Key terms to remember
  • Impediment removal: Addressing technical hurdles or organizational conflicts that obstruct the team's progress.
Slide 12 of 18 · Open in PDF
Easy explanation

Agile coaching helps the team and organization understand practices such as self-organization and continuous improvement, then apply them in their work.

Example: After a sprint, the team can discuss one change that would improve its workflow.

Key terms to remember
  • Agile coaching: Helping the team, Product Owner, and organization understand self-organization and continuous improvement.
Slide 13 of 18 · Open in PDF
Easy explanation

The Development Team collectively builds a potentially releasable, Done increment by the end of each Sprint.

Example: A sprint might produce a working, tested password-reset feature.

Key terms to remember
  • Development Team delivery: Collective responsibility for a potentially releasable, Done product increment at each Sprint's end.
Slide 14 of 18 · Open in PDF
Easy explanation

A self-organizing team chooses how to reach its Sprint Goals and manages its own tasks. This encourages shared ownership and adaptable delivery.

Example: Team members decide how to divide testing and implementation work.

Key terms to remember
  • Self-organizing team: A team that manages its own work and decides how best to achieve Sprint Goals.
  • Functional increments: Working product additions delivered continuously through the team's managed tasks and workflow.
Slide 15 of 18 · Open in PDF
Easy explanation

Cross-functional means the team collectively has the skills needed to complete backlog work, such as coding, testing, and design. This can reduce reliance on outside groups.

Example: A feature team with developers, testing knowledge, and UI/UX skills can take a feature through delivery.

Key terms to remember
  • Cross-functional team: A team collectively possessing the skills needed to turn backlog items into a shippable increment with fewer external dependencies.
Slide 16 of 18 · Open in PDF
Easy explanation

The roles complement one another: the Product Owner guides value, the Development Team creates the product, and the Scrum Master supports collaboration and flow.

Example: Feedback changes priorities, the team adjusts its work, and the Scrum Master helps remove resulting blockers.

Key terms to remember
  • Role synergy: The Product Owner guides value, developers handle execution and quality, and the Scrum Master supports facilitation and flow.
Slide 17 of 18 · Open in PDF
Easy explanation

This reflection asks how the three roles’ cooperation helps a project adapt and work efficiently when conditions are uncertain.

Example: A stakeholder change can be clarified by the Product Owner, planned by the team, and supported by the Scrum Master.

Slide 18 of 18 · Open in PDF
Easy explanation

The conclusion summarizes how distinct but connected roles help Agile teams adapt and deliver value continuously.
