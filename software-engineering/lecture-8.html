This lesson explains how Agile team roles collaborate to create value through iterative work.

Easy explanation

The agenda previews Agile collaboration and the three role areas: Product Owner, Scrum Master, and Development Team.

Example: As you follow the lesson, notice how each role contributes a different kind of support.

Slide 3 of 14 · Open in PDF
Easy explanation

Agile works in short, iterative cycles with frequent collaboration and improvement. Waterfall is presented as a more linear sequence of phases.

Example: An Agile team can deliver a small usable feature, gather feedback, and refine the next one.

Key terms to remember
  • Agile approach: Iterative development with rapid cycles, collaboration, and continuous improvement.
  • Waterfall contrast: Traditional linear, sequential development phases contrasted with Agile's repeated cycles.
Slide 4 of 14 · Open in PDF
Easy explanation

The Product Owner guides value and the backlog; the Scrum Master supports the process and removes obstacles; the Development Team builds the increment.

Example: For a shopping app, the PO prioritizes checkout improvements, the SM clears a test-environment blocker, and developers implement them.

Key terms to remember
  • Product Owner: The role maximizing product value and representing stakeholder needs through Product Backlog management.
Slide 5 of 14 · Open in PDF
Easy explanation

The Product Owner represents customer and business needs and is accountable for maximizing product value. They maintain priorities so development effort serves the product direction.

Example: If users struggle to sign in, the PO may prioritize a simpler sign-in flow over a lower-value visual change.

Key terms to remember
  • Product Backlog prioritization: Ordering product work to reflect business and customer value and guide development direction.
Slide 6 of 14 · Open in PDF
Easy explanation

The PO organizes and prioritizes backlog work, clarifies user stories and acceptance criteria, and engages stakeholders to keep the product aligned with needs.

Example: A story might say “As a student, I can reset my password”; criteria specify a reset link and confirmation.

Key terms to remember
  • User stories and acceptance criteria: Clear descriptions of needed work and conditions of acceptance defined by the Product Owner in this lesson.
  • Stakeholder alignment: Product Owner engagement that connects development efforts to market and stakeholder needs.
Slide 7 of 14 · Open in PDF
Easy explanation

The Scrum Master is a servant-leader who helps the team use Scrum well, removes impediments, and coaches ongoing improvement.

Example: If approvals block progress, the Scrum Master helps the team find a workable route through the process.

Key terms to remember
  • Scrum Master: A servant-leader who facilitates Scrum, removes impediments, and coaches Agile principles.
Slide 8 of 14 · Open in PDF
Easy explanation

The Scrum Master facilitates Scrum events, supports the rules and values, removes obstacles, and helps the team become self-organizing.

Example: They help a retrospective stay focused on one concrete improvement the team can try.

Key terms to remember
  • Scrum rules and values: Process principles supported by the Scrum Master through facilitation and coaching.
Slide 9 of 14 · Open in PDF
Easy explanation

The Development Team is self-organizing and cross-functional: members coordinate their work and bring the skills needed to produce an increment. The slide describes a typically small group.

Example: Design, coding, and testing skills work together to finish a feature within a Sprint.

Key terms to remember
  • Self-organization: Team ownership of how to plan and execute work rather than relying on imposed task-by-task control.
  • Cross-functionality: A combination of professional skills within the Development Team needed to build a complete product increment.
  • Potentially releasable increment: A quality product addition that the Development Team is responsible for delivering each Sprint.
Slide 10 of 14 · Open in PDF
Easy explanation

The team plans and carries out Sprint work together, integrates what it builds, tests for quality, and shares responsibility for technical excellence and delivery.

Example: Developers coordinate implementation and testing rather than handing an unfinished feature over at the end.

Key terms to remember
  • Collective technical ownership: Developers jointly designing, building, integrating, and testing work while owning technical excellence and delivery.
Slide 11 of 14 · Open in PDF
Easy explanation

The roles complement one another: the Product Owner answers what is valuable, the Development Team decides how to build it, and the Scrum Master supports the process.

Example: For a prioritized feature, the PO explains the outcome, developers plan the solution, and the SM helps remove process obstacles.

Key terms to remember
  • What / How / Process role mapping: The Product Owner guides what and value, developers determine how to build, and the Scrum Master facilitates the process.
Slide 12 of 14 · Open in PDF
Easy explanation

This discussion prompt asks how role responsibilities might adapt in very lean or specialized organizations. It encourages applying the role principles thoughtfully as teams change.

Example: A small team may share some tasks, while still making value, delivery, and process responsibilities clear.
