Easy explanation

Synergy describes how individual contributions combine into stronger collective results. Collaboration is a practical way to improve team and project performance.

Example: Two engineers with different experience can identify a solution neither had considered alone.

Key terms to remember
  • Team synergy: Diverse individual contributions combining into stronger collective outcomes through collaboration.
Slide 4 of 14 · Open in PDF
Easy explanation

Working together can speed delivery, reveal problems earlier, and bring different perspectives that lead to creative solutions.

Example: A short design discussion exposes a missing edge case before implementation is complete.

Key terms to remember
  • Collaborative efficiency and innovation: Working together to resolve problems faster and use diverse perspectives to produce creative solutions.
Slide 5 of 14 · Open in PDF
Easy explanation

Code reviews find defects early and give developers useful feedback. Sharing knowledge and ownership supports learning and more robust code.

Example: A reviewer notices an unchecked empty input and explains a safe validation pattern.

Key terms to remember
  • Code reviews: Peer evaluation that finds defects early and provides constructive learning opportunities.
  • Collective ownership: Shared responsibility and knowledge that support robust software and continuous improvement.
Slide 6 of 14 · Open in PDF
Easy explanation

Teams should make room for different viewpoints and challenge assumptions respectfully. Co-creation means developing a solution together, building shared understanding and ownership along the way.

Example: Before choosing a database design, teammates compare trade-offs and combine the strongest ideas.

Key terms to remember
  • Expressing differences: Openly sharing varied viewpoints and challenging assumptions to reveal flaws and improve solutions.
  • Co-creation: Jointly constructing solutions and knowledge instead of developing entirely in individual silos.
Slide 7 of 14 · Open in PDF
Easy explanation

Mutual alignment means everyone understands the goals, scope, and progress. Documentation preserves important knowledge so people can transfer and maintain it over time.

Example: A short API reference helps a new teammate use a service without relying on its original author.

Key terms to remember
  • Mutual alignment: A shared understanding of goals, scope, and progress that reduces misunderstandings and improves coordination.
  • Comprehensive documentation: Accessible comments, design specifications, API references, and guides that preserve institutional knowledge and aid onboarding.
Slide 8 of 14 · Open in PDF
Easy explanation

Formal knowledge sharing is scheduled and structured, such as a workshop. Informal sharing happens in everyday interactions, such as pair programming or a quick question. Both spread skills and solve problems.

Example: A monthly tech talk is formal; asking a teammate to pair on a failing test is informal.

Key terms to remember
  • Formal knowledge sharing: Planned sessions such as workshops, presentations, internal tech talks, and lunch-and-learn events.
  • Informal knowledge sharing: Ad-hoc support through quick chats, pair programming, and questions in communication channels.
Slide 9 of 14 · Open in PDF
Easy explanation

Trello uses visual Kanban boards; Asana supports task and project planning; Jira is oriented toward software issue tracking and configurable workflows.

Example: A small team might track cards on a Trello board, while a larger software project uses Jira issue workflows.

Key terms to remember
  • Trello: A collaboration tool using Kanban boards for visual task and progress management.
  • Asana: A project and task management tool with varied views, resource planning, and workflow automation.
  • Jira: An agile development tool specializing in issue tracking, workflow customization, and reporting.
Slide 10 of 14 · Open in PDF
Easy explanation

Open communication builds trust and clarifies expectations. Sharing information transparently helps people stay aligned and avoid preventable misunderstandings.

Example: Post a brief decision summary after a meeting so absent teammates know what changed.

Key terms to remember
  • Open communication: Transparent dialogue that builds trust, clarifies expectations, and prevents misunderstandings.
Slide 11 of 14 · Open in PDF
Easy explanation

Distributed teams may face time-zone delays, cultural differences, weaker cohesion, and knowledge silos. Agendas, asynchronous channels, team-building, and clear documentation can help.

Example: Record a decision in a shared channel so teammates across time zones can respond when available.

Key terms to remember
  • Distributed team challenges: Time-zone communication delays, cultural differences, cohesion difficulties, and knowledge silos.
  • Distributed team strategies: Structured agendas, virtual channels, asynchronous updates, team-building, and transparent documentation.
